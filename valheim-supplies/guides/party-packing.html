Field note / Party packing

A shared chest is not a packed bag.

There are three different numbers in a good supply plan: what each person should take, what the party already owns, and the ingredients available for the remaining work. Keeping them separate prevents the easiest mistake: treating the same item as available in two places.

Start with one player’s pack.

Agree on the amount of each supply a player should carry. Enter that amount in “Pack per person,” then enter the number of players. Four players taking 20 fire arrows each need 80 arrows altogether. The planner does not judge whether 20 arrows is enough for your route.

Set a supply to zero if you do not want it. Selecting both arrow types means packing both, not substituting one for the other. The three listed foods are an available recipe subset, not a recommendation to use that combination for every biome.

Count finished items once.

Put the party’s total accessible finished stock in the corresponding stock field. If one player has 30 arrows and a shared chest holds 50, enter 80 only if all 80 can actually be redistributed. Do not enter the total for each player.

For mead, count finished bottles. A base waiting in a fermenter is neither a finished bottle nor a collection of raw ingredients. This version does not model bases already made or fermenting. Wait until those bottles are ready before counting them as finished stock, or account for that production separately.

Reserve ingredients honestly.

The optional ingredient inventory is a single pool shared by all selected recipes. Blueberries used by jam and healing mead are added together before your stock is subtracted. Enter only ingredients available to this plan, not materials promised to a different project.

A useful handoff: one person gathers the combined missing-ingredient list, another handles the station batches, and everyone receives the same per-person packing list. Newly crafted extras can stay in the shared chest instead of being mistaken for part of the original target.

A quantity plan is not a survival guarantee.

This tool does not simulate eating, effects, carrying weight, inventory slots, combat, travel, or spoilage. Food and mead durations are not used to choose quantities. Production takes time and requires the listed stations; station construction and fuel are outside the ingredient totals. Cooking-skill bonuses may give extra output, so the planner uses base recipe yields instead of promising a skill-dependent return.
