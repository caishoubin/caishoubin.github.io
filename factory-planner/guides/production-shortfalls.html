NOTE 003 / DIAGNOSIS

Diagnose production shortages before your buffer runs dry

A full container can make an undersupplied factory look healthy. Watch the rate of change, not just the amount in the box.

INDEPENDENT FAN TOOL · SOLID-ITEM LOGISTICS · UPDATED 20 OCT 2026

When consumers keep working, it is tempting to assume the production chain is balanced. Storage can make that assumption look true for a long time. The key measurement is net flow: actual incoming items minus outgoing demand. A negative net rate is a countdown, even while the container still looks comfortably full.

Calculate the trend

Net inventory rate = actual incoming rate − outgoing rate
Time to empty = current stock ÷ (outgoing rate − incoming rate)

The time-to-empty formula applies only when output exceeds input. If input exceeds output, the container fills instead. If the rates match, the model predicts no change in inventory. Equal rates do not create a reserve or restore items consumed during a previous interruption.

Worked example: a slow, hidden deficit

A buffer has 2,400 items. Actual input is 240 items/min and downstream machines need 360 items/min. The shortfall is 120 items/min, so the reserve lasts 2,400 ÷ 120 = 20 minutes.

Doubling the stocked reserve to 4,800 items would give 40 minutes. The extra storage changes the deadline; it does not change the underlying 120-item/min deficit.

Locate the missing flow

Work backwards from the consumer and ask three questions. First, is the outgoing route capable of delivering the requested flow? Second, is enough supply reaching the buffer? Third, are the producers actually making the amount their configuration suggests?

  1. Transport-limited: available production is sufficient, but a serial belt or shared merger output is too slow. Improve the constrained connection or preserve enough independent routes.
  2. Production-limited: the available producer output is below demand even though the belt has spare capacity. Inspect ingredients, machine operation and the production plan.
  3. Temporarily interrupted: normal sustainable input meets demand, but a bounded stop or startup period causes a short dip. A correctly stocked buffer can help if there is a way to recharge it afterward.

Same symptoms, different fixes

Case A: supply 600, demand 600, Mk.4 route. The route can deliver only 480 items/min. Transport is the immediate bottleneck.

Case B: supply 480, demand 600, Mk.5 route. The route has room for 780, but delivery still cannot exceed 480. More belt capacity is not the missing ingredient.

Case C: supply 700, demand 600, Mk.4 route. The 480-capacity route hides the production surplus. Once the transport limit is removed, up to 100 items/min can replenish storage after demand is met.

Do not confuse a manifold warming up with balance

A manifold distributes flow along successive splitters. Its early behavior can differ from its settled state as upstream machine inventories fill. A machine farther down the line may be starved during startup even when the final steady-state plan has sufficient supply and belt capacity.

Observe the system after a reasonable warm-up and inspect whether upstream inventories are still changing. A short observation is not enough to prove balance. Equally, waiting longer cannot repair a permanent aggregate production shortage.

Use actual incoming flow for storage calculations. If machines nominally produce 600 items/min but only 480 can pass through the incoming route, enter 480 as the buffer input. Entering the nominal total would predict reserve growth that cannot happen.

What to do when a buffer is draining

Read the estimated time to empty as a planning window. Reduce demand temporarily if needed, diagnose the constrained segment, then change the supply or transport capacity that is actually limiting flow. After the fix, confirm a positive net rate if you need the reserve to recover.

If the measured inventory trend disagrees with the calculator, revisit the assumptions. Rates may not be constant; some items may be going elsewhere; or the selected storage may not be reachable at the requested output rate. This tool is a transparent accounting model, not a simulation of the entire factory.
