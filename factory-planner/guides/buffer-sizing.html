NOTE 002 / STORAGE

Size a Satisfactory storage buffer for outages

A useful buffer starts with a duration: how long do you need the downstream factory to keep running without new input?

INDEPENDENT FAN TOOL · SOLID-ITEM LOGISTICS · UPDATED 21 OCT 2026

A storage container can smooth a temporary interruption, but its physical size is only half the story. You also need enough stock inside it and an exit route that can deliver at the required rate. An empty industrial container is additional space, not additional reserve.

Start with the interruption you want to cover

For a complete interruption of incoming flow, multiply downstream demand by the outage duration. Keep the units consistent: items per minute must be multiplied by minutes. Convert 90 seconds to 1.5 minutes before calculating. Round the final stock requirement up to a whole item.

Required reserve = ceil(outgoing items/min × outage minutes)
Current coverage = stored items ÷ outgoing items/min

Worked example: a 90-second stop

A line needs 360 items/min. You want it to keep running through a 90-second interruption. The target reserve is ceil(360 × 1.5) = 540 items.

If you currently have 400 items, coverage is 400 ÷ 360 = 1.11 minutes, or about 67 seconds. A large container does not change that result until you actually add the missing 140 items.

Convert slots to the right item capacity

A standard Storage Container has 24 slots; an Industrial Storage Container has 48. Capacity in items depends on the stack size of the specific item you store. This tool deliberately asks for your stack size rather than assuming that every material stacks alike.

Usable capacity = available slots × item stack size

With a stack size of 100, a standard container holds 2,400 items and an industrial container holds 4,800. If other item types occupy some slots, use only the slots available to this material. Multiple containers can be added together only when their contents are accessible to the intended output route.

Check the exit before trusting the reserve. A box with 2,400 items cannot keep 360 items/min of demand supplied through a single Mk.2 exit rated at 120 items/min. Storage volume does not remove an output bottleneck.

Allow enough surplus to recharge

After input returns, stock rebuilds only when the incoming rate exceeds the outgoing rate. If input and output are equal, production may resume normally, but the reserve stays at its reduced level. Recovery needs a temporary reduction in demand or a sustained surplus of incoming items.

Worked example: rebuilding 540 items

Normal input is 420 items/min and demand is 360 items/min. The surplus is 60 items/min. Replacing 540 consumed reserve items takes 540 ÷ 60 = 9 minutes, assuming constant rates and enough space.

If another outage starts before recharge is complete, the second interruption has less protection. Choose your reserve and recovery margin together.

Read the reserve and recovery results

“Build planned reserve now” estimates how long it takes to reach the outage target from today’s stock while normal demand continues. “Restore pre-outage stock” starts after the outage ends and replaces only the stock actually consumed. It is not the same as time to fill every storage slot.

For 540 items in storage, input of 420/min, output of 360/min and a 90-second outage, you are ready now. The outage uses all 540 items, then the 60/min surplus rebuilds them in nine minutes. With only 400 items to start, the outage leaves 140 items of demand unserved. Refilling those 400 consumed items takes about 6.67 minutes, but preparing the full 540-item reserve for the next outage still takes nine minutes. The calculator does not pretend a refill recovers missed production.

When input is equal to or below output, depleted stock cannot recharge at the entered rates. When the target exceeds storage capacity, the reserve is unreachable until capacity is increased.

Understand the calculator’s two clocks

“Minutes to full” or “minutes to empty” uses both of your normal rates. “Full-outage coverage” assumes incoming flow suddenly becomes zero while the outgoing demand stays unchanged. These are different scenarios, so their time estimates will usually be different.

For example, with 2,400 stored items, normal input of 240 and output of 360, the buffer drains at 120 items/min and empties in 20 minutes. If input stops entirely, the same stock lasts only 2,400 ÷ 360 = 6.67 minutes.

Choose an honest safety margin

The result is an ideal, continuous-flow estimate. It does not model delayed restarts, uneven distribution, inaccessible storage, machine cycles or interruptions longer than expected. Add a margin that fits the reliability you need, and verify the line in the game. Do not label a chosen margin as a guaranteed game mechanic.

Mechanic reference: Storage Container on the official community wiki. Industrial storage should not be treated as a guaranteed equal-output splitter.
