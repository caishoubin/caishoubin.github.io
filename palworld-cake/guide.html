From shelf to gathering list

Updated October 21, 2026 · Normal Cake reference snapshot

The most useful number is not a recipe multiplied by a target. It is the gap between that recipe and the ingredients you can actually use.

Start with a ten-cake target

Suppose you want 10 cakes in total and already have 3 ready. Enter 10 as the target and 3 as ready cakes. Your new batch is 7 cakes, not 10. Ready cakes have already consumed their ingredients, so do not subtract those ingredients from your pantry a second time.

For this example, your available stock is 10 flour, 16 wheat, 40 red berries, 100 milk, 50 eggs and 12 honey. Only count ingredients that are still available and not reserved for something else.

Seven Normal Cakes require: 35 flour, 56 red berries, 49 milk, 56 eggs and 14 honey.

That is the recipe total. It is not the gathering list yet.

Let your flour do the first part

You already own 10 of the 35 flour required. That leaves a 25-flour gap before milling. Sixteen wheat can make 5 whole flour at a rate of 3 wheat per flour, using 15 wheat and leaving 1 wheat.

After that milling step, your batch still needs 20 flour. The planner does not treat the leftover wheat as a fraction of usable flour: one wheat is not enough for another milling recipe.

Choose one route for the remaining flour

If you choose Gather flour, the list asks for 20 flour, 16 red berries, 6 eggs and 2 honey. Milk is already covered. The owned-wheat milling step is still required; the flour route credits those 5 flour first.

If you choose Gather wheat & mill, the list asks for 59 wheat, 16 red berries, 6 eggs and 2 honey. Why 59, rather than 60? The 1 wheat left after milling is still yours: add 59 and you can mill the remaining 20 flour.

Both routes solve the same shortage. Never combine the 20-flour and 59-wheat amounts into one shopping list. Gathering a full flour deficit as well as its wheat equivalent would double count it.

Capacity is different from your target

With those ingredients, you can supply 2 cakes immediately because 10 flour covers 2 recipes. Milling your owned wheat increases your flour to 15 and your ingredient capacity to 3 cakes. Both numbers exclude the 3 cakes already made.

The capacity cards are not capped at the batch target. They describe what your ingredients could support. The sentence under them tells you how many of the 7 still-needed cakes are covered. If several ingredients tie at the lowest recipe count, all of them are shown.

Before you leave the base

  1. Check that your counts are individual items, not stacks.
  2. Keep flour and unprocessed wheat separate. Do not enter the flour you hope to mill as stock you already own.
  3. Select one gathering route, then copy or download the list.
  4. After gathering and milling, update your stock manually. The planner is a snapshot, not a live inventory connection.

The numbers only establish ingredient coverage. You still need suitable in-game facilities and work to turn ingredients into cakes. This guide makes no promise about cooking speed, spoilage or breeding outcomes.
