Good numbers, no hidden ingredients

Updated October 21, 2026 · Normal Cake reference snapshot

A deliberately small model: one Normal Cake recipe, one wheat-to-flour conversion, and your own inventory counts.

1. Deduct ready cakes first

Cakes still to make = the larger of zero and (target cakes − ready cakes).

The target is the total you want, not the number of additional cakes to cook. A ready count above the target creates a surplus, never a negative ingredient requirement.

2. Apply the recipe, then deduct stock

For each ingredient, required quantity is cakes still to make multiplied by its amount per cake. The normal recipe uses 5 flour, 8 red berries, 7 milk, 8 eggs and 2 honey for 1 cake.

Missing before milling = the larger of zero and (required quantity − on-hand quantity).

The expandable ingredient table shows this pre-milling view. Flour is then processed by the additional steps below; berries, milk, eggs and honey have no modeled subrecipes.

3. Mill only useful owned wheat

Three wheat make one flour. Divide owned wheat by 3 and round down to get how much whole flour you can mill. The milling step for this target is the smaller of that amount and the flour shortage. Multiply it by 3 to see how much owned wheat to use.

Remaining flour shortage = pre-milling flour shortage − useful flour milled from owned wheat.

Additional wheat to gather instead = the larger of zero and (3 × pre-milling flour shortage − all owned wheat).

That last formula credits every owned wheat, including a remainder of 1 or 2. For example, if you own 4 flour and 2 wheat and need 1 cake, you cannot supply it yet. But gathering 1 more wheat completes the milling recipe and closes the gap. Gathering 3 more would be unnecessary.

If the batch needs only 50 flour and you own 300 wheat, the batch milling step uses 150 wheat, not all 300. Extra stock is not automatically consumed.

4. Keep capacity separate

For each of the five ingredients, divide on-hand quantity by its recipe amount and round down. The smallest result is the maximum whole-cake ingredient capacity. Every ingredient tied at that minimum is labeled as limiting.

Capacity now uses flour already in your pantry. After milling owned wheat uses owned flour plus the whole flour obtainable from all owned wheat. This second capacity can exceed what the current target needs, while the target-specific milling step never mills more than the batch requires.

Neither capacity includes ready cakes, future gathering or cooking time. The progress bar is target coverage after owned-wheat milling, capped at 100%.

Boundaries of the model

  • All inputs are whole item counts from 0 to 1,000,000. Blank, negative, fractional and non-finite values are rejected, not silently rounded.
  • Only Normal Cake is modeled. Other cake variants, mods and custom recipes are outside this catalog.
  • No work speed, farming schedule, machine throughput, breeding mechanics, spoilage, storage access or world settings are simulated.
  • Inventory is entered manually. Editing a plan does not change the game or update other browser tabs.
  • Shared plans use schema version 1 and the normal-cake-2026-10 catalog. Unknown versions, missing fields, extra fields and malformed values are rejected.
