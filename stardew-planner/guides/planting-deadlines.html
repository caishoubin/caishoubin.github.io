Field note 01 · The season clock

The last day is closer than you think.

The easiest crop mistake in Stardew Valley is not choosing the wrong seed. It is counting the day you plant as a full day of growth.

Last reviewed October 20, 2026 · Independent fan resource

A normal outdoor season has 28 days. To harvest before it ends, your crop needs enough overnight growth steps between planting and its first harvest. Last Seed starts with that calendar, then adds the budget question. Getting the calendar right comes first: an attractive sale price is not useful if the crop never becomes ready.

Think of planting day as day zero

Plant a four-day parsnip on Spring 1 and water it that day. Its first harvest is Spring 5. Spring 1 is the setup day; four growing days take you to the fifth. This is why adding one to a deadline can quietly ruin an entire planting.

First harvest = planting day + growth days

Last planting day = 28 − growth days

These formulas assume normal growth speed, watering every day, and harvesting as soon as the crop is ready.

A six-day potato planted on Spring 22 is ready on Spring 28. Plant the same potato on Spring 23 and its first harvest would fall after the season. For the single-season crops included here, that is too late for an ordinary outdoor planting.

Useful deadlines to keep in your head

  • Parsnip: four growth days, so Spring 24 is the last planting day.
  • Potato and kale: six growth days, so Spring 22 is the last day.
  • Cauliflower: twelve growth days, so Spring 16 is the last day.
  • Melon: twelve growth days, so Summer 16 is the last day.
  • Pumpkin: thirteen growth days, so Fall 15 is the last day.
  • Cranberries: seven growth days, so Fall 21 is the last day for one harvest, though that late planting does not recover the standard seed price at minimum base-quality yield.

Those dates describe the final possible first harvest under the model, not necessarily a sensible buying day. The crop can be technically ready while still being a poor use of gold. That distinction matters most for expensive regrowing seeds.

A buffer is worth more than a perfect deadline

The listed deadline leaves no room for a missed watering day. If you plant at the boundary, a delay can push the harvest outside the season. In a real save, your watering capacity, available energy, and daily routine may matter more than fitting every possible tile.

A practical approach is to treat the mathematical deadline as a hard edge and your own earlier target as the comfortable choice. Last Seed does not invent a universal buffer because your farm is different from someone else’s. If you want one extra day of room, plant one day before the shown deadline.

Repeat harvests have a second clock

Once a regrowing crop produces its first harvest, its regrowth period determines later dates. Blueberries planted on Summer 1 take thirteen days to the first harvest: Summer 14. At four days between harvests, the remaining dates are Summer 18, 22, and 26.

Notice what does not happen: the plant does not restart its original thirteen-day growth period. You also do not pay for a fresh seed after each harvest. A single-harvest crop works differently. Last Seed stops after that crop’s one harvest; it does not assume you immediately purchase and plant another seed.

Know when this shortcut stops applying

The 28-minus-growth rule here is for the selected single-season outdoor crops at normal speed. It is not a universal rule for every farm location or crop. Greenhouse growing, Ginger Island, crops that survive across multiple seasons, fertilizer, professions, and modded calendars need additional logic. They are deliberately outside this tool.

When in doubt, check the crop’s own page and the conditions in your save. A simple model is helpful when its boundaries are clear.
