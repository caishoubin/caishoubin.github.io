About the project

A small tool for a better growing day.

Last Seed helps answer a small, practical question: if I plant this crop today, what can I harvest before the season ends?

Last reviewed October 20, 2026 · Independent fan resource

This is an independent Stardew Valley fan project published by caishoubin on GitHub. It is not affiliated with ConcernedApe, the game’s publisher, or the Stardew Valley Wiki. Game names and trademarks belong to their respective owners. The writing, interface, and simple garden illustrations here are original to this site.

What the planner actually models

You buy seeds for one crop, plant them once on the selected day, water every day, and harvest as soon as they are ready. A single-harvest crop is not replanted. A regrowing crop stays in the ground and produces again whenever its regrowth timer allows. The model ends on day 28.

This distinction matters. A quick crop can fund later plantings, but that reinvestment is outside this model. A comparison here is a comparison of one initial sowing, not a recommendation for the most profitable possible season.

Included

15 selected spring, summer, and fall crops; standard outdoor 28-day seasons; normal growth; daily watering; immediate harvesting; Pierre’s standard prices; 100% profit margin; base-quality raw crops; minimum item yields.

Outside the model

Replanting, fertilizer, growth boosts, professions, missed watering, crop destruction, random bonus yields, quality bonuses, giant crops, processing, seed makers, greenhouse, Ginger Island, multi-season crops, and winter growing.

The arithmetic

  • First harvest: planting day + growth days. The planting day is day zero.
  • Last planting day: 28 − growth days.
  • Harvest count: zero if first harvest is after day 28. Otherwise one, plus the number of full regrowth periods that fit before or on day 28.
  • Plants: the smaller of your plot count and your budget divided by seed price, rounded down.
  • Seed spending: plants × seed price.
  • Gross sales: plants × harvest count × minimum items per harvest × base sale price.
  • Net after seeds: gross sales − seed spending. Only seeds are deducted.
  • Cash left after seeds: budget − seed spending. Harvest income has not arrived yet.

When it is too late to grow a crop, the tool still shows the cost of buying those seeds. That makes the potential mistake visible: zero harvests does not mean the purchase was free. Zero plots or an unaffordable seed produces zero plants and zero spending.

Where the numbers come from

The starting reference is the Stardew Valley Wiki crop guide, checked against crop pages for seed price, growth time, regrowth, normal sale price, and minimum yield. Every selected crop has a direct source link in the planner. These are standard 1.6-era mechanics; this site does not claim verification for a later major game version or mods.

For example, a blueberry harvest is modeled as three berries, and a cranberry harvest as two. Extra random items are excluded. The potato model likewise excludes additional random potatoes. The estimate is intentionally conservative about yield, but actual farm conditions can still cause lower returns.

Editorial approach and corrections

Guides explain the decisions behind the numbers, with worked examples that can be reproduced in the calculator. We avoid presenting gross revenue as profit or labeling one crop universally best. If you find a mismatch, compare the stated assumptions with the linked crop source and the game version you are using. The publisher’s public repository provides the source and its available contribution channels.
